title: Manage Inventory
description: Check available stock, adjust quantities, and see what is already allocated.
---


The **Inventory** page shows each product option separately. Use it to decide what you can sell now, what is waiting for pickup, and what you may need to order.

## Read inventory quantities

Search by product, variant, or SKU. The table shows:

| Column | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Free** | On-hand stock not currently reserved for a sale. |
| **On Hand** | Current stock count. |
| **Allocated** | Stock reserved for a member but not yet handed over. |
| **Total Outstanding** | Units on paid, partially paid, or refunded invoices that have neither an allocation nor a fulfillment record. Allocated units are listed separately. |
| **Shortfall** | Outstanding units that exceed the current on-hand count. |

An item can have stock on hand but no free stock if all units are allocated. Check **Allocated** and **Free** before promising an item to someone else. **Shortfall** compares Total Outstanding with On Hand, not Free, so it does not account for existing reservations in the same way.

For example, if you have 5 belts on hand and 2 have been allocated for pickup, **Free** is 3 and **Allocated** is 2. A paid order for 4 more belts that cannot yet be filled contributes to **Total Outstanding**. If the outstanding demand is greater than the current stock, **Shortfall** helps show how many units you still need.

## Adjust stock

Use an adjustment to correct a count or record a change that did not come through a sale, return, or purchase order.

1. Open **Products** and select the product.
2. Find the variant and select its **Adjust Inventory** action.
3. Enter a positive quantity to add stock or a negative quantity to remove stock.
4. Add a reason so your team can understand the change later.
5. Select **Adjust Inventory**.

The quantity must be nonzero and cannot reduce on-hand stock below zero.

Use adjustments for a stocktake correction, damaged stock, or another change that was not already recorded by a shop transaction. For example, enter **-1** with a reason such as "damaged during storage" to remove one unsellable item. Do not use a manual adjustment to record a supplier delivery or a customer return. Those have their own workflows and keep the supplier or sale history connected to the quantity change.

Open **Inventory**, then **Adjustments** to review previous changes. The list identifies the product, variant, adjustment type, quantity, supplier when relevant, reason, and date. You can export the adjustments table to CSV. Use [Purchase Orders](/shop/purchase-orders/) to add stock received from a supplier.

## Handle low or reserved stock

When a member has paid for an item that is not available, the invoice shows that the item is waiting for stock. Order the missing quantity using a [purchase order](/shop/purchase-orders/). When stock arrives, allocate it to waiting sales before making the remaining stock available for general sale.

Before creating an order, compare **Shortfall** with the quantity you plan to buy. A shortfall reflects current outstanding demand, not a recommended order quantity: you may want extra stock for expected sales, or less if you are discontinuing an item. Receiving stock through the purchase order updates on-hand quantities; allocating units to waiting invoices reserves them for those customers.

For sale handovers, see [Fulfill Product Sales](/shop/fulfil-sales/). To review product costs and sales, see [Shop Reports](/shop/reports/).
