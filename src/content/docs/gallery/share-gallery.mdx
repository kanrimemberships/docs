---
title: Share Gallery with Members
description: Use public folders and items to make appropriate school gallery content available to members.
---

Gallery folders and items have a **Public** setting. Use it only for content your school intends members to see. Review your school's photo-sharing permissions before making images public.

Members must sign in and have access to your school to browse the Gallery. **Public** does not create a permanent public download link. If your school uses an automatic gallery on its public website, public school-gallery items with public parent folders may also appear there. Staff with **View Gallery** can see private items too.

## Prepare content for sharing

1. Open **Gallery** and create or open the folder you want to share.
2. Edit the folder and enable **Public**.
3. Upload files into that folder or edit existing items.
4. Enable **Public** for each item members should see.
5. Open the member-facing view and confirm the content is appropriate and visible as intended.

When creating or editing a public item, Kanri requires its folder and every parent folder to be public. Make the parent folders public first, then update the item.

To withdraw a folder's content, turn off **Public** on that folder. Kanri also makes its subfolders and items private. Members cannot bypass a private parent by opening an item's direct view or download link, even if an older item still has **Public** enabled.

:::caution
Changing privacy blocks new access through the Gallery, but previously issued preview or download links can still work until they expire. Gallery file links last up to 24 hours; automatic website-gallery links last up to one hour. Files already saved on someone's device cannot be recalled. Images added separately to your website must also be removed from that website.
:::

## Protect member privacy

Do not publish photos or documents unless your school has permission to share them. Keep sensitive or member-specific records in the member's private gallery or Documents area rather than the school-wide Gallery.
